# SwedeVO (Swedish Voice) Protocol

This is the official doctrine for all internal and external messaging, design documentation, and pitch copy in the workspace. It is the "Steve Voice" / "Swedish Approach."

## 1. State the point. Drop the explanation.
Do not over-explain. Swedish people do not over-explain twice. Say what the thing is, what it does, and move on. If you have to justify it for three paragraphs, the design is too complicated. 
- **BAD:** "The core design replaces traditional line-of-sight stealth with 'Occupancy Fields' and 'Tension Budgets', requiring the player to read the room, manipulate AI behavior, and orchestrate chaos."
- **SWEDEVO:** "You read the room, manipulate targets, and orchestrate chaos."

## 2. No Systemic Bullshit
Keep the language rooted in the player's fantasy and physical actions, not the backend math. The audience doesn't care about the spreadsheet; they care about the consequence. Jargon dilutes the impact.
- **BAD:** "Stumbling over your words will bleed your Social Capital and bloat the room's Suspicion Budget."
- **SWEDEVO:** "Stumbling over your words raises scrutiny."

## 3. Direct, Punchy, Blue-Collar Pacing
Cut the multi-clause sentences. Use periods. Short, aggressive sentences carry more weight and confidence than long, flowery ones.
- **BAD:** "As the primary arteries of the estate lock down, spatial intelligence becomes your lifeline, allowing you to slip through a servant’s corridor to outflank the heavily armed brutes."
- **SWEDEVO:** "As the estate locks down, spatial intelligence is your lifeline. Slipping through a servant’s corridor lets you outflank the brutes. A gentleman knows the exits."

## 4. Consequence over Mechanics
When describing a feature, describe what happens in the game world, not what happens in the code.
- **BAD:** "If you trigger a fail state during a stealth section, the aggro radius increases."
- **SWEDEVO:** "If you botch a quiet approach, the encounter devolves into a desperate grapple on the floor."

## Summary
The SwedeVO is confident. It doesn't need to over-justify itself. 
State the point. Drop the explanation. Move on.
